How to Size a Solar System for Your Home

A step by step method for working out the inverter, battery and panels your home needs, with a worked example for a typical Lagos flat.

Cloud Energy Engineering Team7 min read

Most solar systems that disappoint their owners were sized by guesswork. The inverter trips when the fridge starts, the battery is flat by 2am, or the panels never fully recharge the battery. Sizing properly takes four steps, and you can do the first three yourself before you speak to an engineer.

Step 1: List what you want to power

Walk through your home and write down every appliance you want on solar, its power rating in watts (printed on the label or in the manual) and how many hours a day it runs. Be honest about what runs at the same time, because that sets the inverter size.

ApplianceQuantityWatts eachHours per dayEnergy per day
LED bulbs610W6360Wh
Ceiling fans260W101,200Wh
Television and decoder1120W5600Wh
Refrigerator1150W10 (compressor run time)1,500Wh
Wi-Fi router115W24360Wh
Laptop charging265W4520Wh
Total4,540Wh

In this example the household uses about 4.5kWh a day, and the largest load running at one time is roughly 700W when everything is on together.

Step 2: Choose the inverter

The inverter must carry everything that runs at once, with headroom. Add about 25% to your simultaneous load, and allow for surge: motors in fridges, freezers, pumps and air conditioners draw two to three times their running power for a moment when they start.

  • Simultaneous load of 700W plus 25% headroom gives about 875W.
  • The fridge compressor can surge to around 450W on start up.
  • A 2kVA inverter covers this comfortably. A 1kVA unit would trip whenever the fridge starts while other loads are on.
Engineer's tip

If you plan to add a freezer, pumping machine or air conditioner later, size the inverter for that now. Replacing an inverter costs more than buying the right one first.

Step 3: Size the battery

Work out how much energy you need from the battery when there is no sun and no grid, usually overnight. In the example, about 3kWh of the daily 4.5kWh is used between 6pm and 7am.

Lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) batteries can safely deliver 80 to 90% of their rated capacity, so a 3kWh night needs at least 3.5 to 4kWh of battery. A 25.6V 200Ah Cloud Energy lithium battery stores 5.12kWh, which covers the night with a margin for cloudy days and battery ageing.

Step 4: Size the solar panels

Your panels must replace the energy you use each day, plus losses in wiring, charging and heat. Lagos receives roughly 4 to 5 peak sun hours a day on average, fewer during the rainy season and harmattan.

  1. Daily energy: 4.5kWh.
  2. Divide by peak sun hours: 4.5kWh ÷ 4.5h = 1kW of panels in perfect conditions.
  3. Allow about 25% for losses: 1kW ÷ 0.8 = 1.25kW.
  4. Round up to whole panels: three 590W bifacial panels give 1.77kW, which also recharges the battery on duller days.

Finally, match a charge controller to the array. Many hybrid inverters include an MPPT controller. For separate inverters, an MPPT controller of 60A to 100A is typical for home systems.

Why an energy audit still matters

The method above gets you close. A site audit checks what a spreadsheet cannot: roof orientation and shading, cable runs, the condition of your wiring, and loads you forgot to list. Cloud Energy engineers carry out audits before every installation so the system is sized to how you actually use power.
